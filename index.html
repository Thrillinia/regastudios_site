The Process

How it unfolds.

I

Getting to Know the Story

We begin with an introductory conversation about the person at the center of the film, the occasion, and what you'd like the finished piece to convey. We'll talk through important people, memories, music, and any moments you'd like us to keep in mind.

Afterward, we'll send a brief summary so you can correct or add anything before we begin.

II

Reviewing and Curating the Photographs

When you're ready, share a broad collection of photographs that could help tell the featured person's story, including:

  • Different ages and stages of their life
  • Key relationships and occasions, with the featured person in focus
  • Milestones, and the everyday moments that capture the featured person's personality

When in doubt, include it. There's no need to choose, sort, or narrow the collection. We'll select the images that best tell the story, weighing significance, variety, and suitability for animation. See the photos that work best.

Please send original image files rather than screenshots or compressed copies from messaging apps. Photos spread across folders, devices, or an external drive are perfectly fine; our photo submission guide explains how to share them.

III

Confirming the Final Selection

We'll gather the proposed photographs into a folder for your review, so you can confirm the right moments and memories are represented.

The selection must be approved at least six weeks before the event. Once approved, it is final, unless we identify a technical or creative issue that requires an adjustment.

IV

Bringing It to Life

With the photographs approved, we begin creating and editing the film, developing the movement, pacing, and transitions that carry the story.

Near completion, we may reconnect about the opening and closing scenes so the film begins and ends in a way that feels right.

V

Reviewing Your Film

You'll receive a draft of your Lifescape to review. Your quoted price includes two rounds of feedback. Here is how it works:

One email per round

One designated family contact gathers all comments and sends them to us in a single email. Please don't circulate the draft widely: it spoils the surprise and can draw out the timeline.

Specific notes

Please identify specific issues at specific moments, ideally with a timestamp. If something feels off, let us know which transition or moment it relates to.

Within the medium

We'll do our best to accommodate every reasonable request, within the capabilities of AI-assisted animation.

A note on AI animation

AI-assisted animation is an evolving creative medium. We work carefully to preserve each person's recognizable appearance and the integrity of the original photographs, but visual inconsistencies can occur during movement. We'll address anything that materially affects a person's appearance or the viewing experience, though inconsistencies and artifacts may remain.

VI

Final Delivery

Once review is complete, we'll deliver your final high-resolution digital film, ready for your event. Where relevant, coordinate with your event production team in advance to confirm playback requirements.

Choosing Photographs

The photos that
come to life best.

A Lifescape tells the story of one person. The strongest photographs keep the focus on them.

01

The featured person, in focus

Choose photos where the featured person is the clear subject: facing the camera or turned toward it, with their face clearly visible.

02

As few people as possible

Solo portraits and photos with one or two others animate most naturally. Large group shots are harder to bring to life, so keep them to the moments that truly matter.

03

In action, with something in hand

Photos of the featured person doing something give us the most to work with: holding a lacrosse stick, playing an instrument, cooking, riding a bike, blowing out birthday candles.

Works beautifully
  • Solo portraits at different ages
  • Mid-activity: sports, hobbies, travel
  • Close moments with one or two loved ones
Harder to animate
  • Large group or crowd photos
  • The featured person small or far away
  • Faces turned away, hidden, or out of focus

Before We Begin

A few things
to keep in mind.

Music is part of the story.

Your song preferences shape the emotional arc of the piece, and we'll discuss your selections during the creative process. Music use is subject to applicable licensing and usage requirements.

Every piece is individual.

The final result depends on the photographs available, their quality, and what's possible in animating them. Our focus is a cohesive, personal experience rather than a perfect recreation of every detail in every frame.
